Crumble

Privacy policy · last updated 2 October 2026

Crumble is an Android app for keeping your recipes: you type them in, import them from a web page or from Paprika, and cook from them. There is also a small web viewer at halfurness.com/crumble that reads a backup you have made.

This policy describes Crumble as it is actually built, not how apps of this kind usually work. It is published by Hal Furness.

The short version

  • Your recipes live on your phone. There are no Crumble accounts and no server of ours, so we never receive your recipes, your photos or anything else you put in the app.
  • Importing a recipe from a web page makes your phone fetch that page directly from the site it is on.
  • Google Drive backup is optional and part of Pro. It copies your recipes into your own Google Drive, where they are not encrypted by Crumble.
  • The app makes two kinds of request to Google on its own: asking Google Play what you have bought, and — unless you have bought Pro — requesting ads. The advertising one collects a substantial amount about your device. It is listed in full below.

What is stored on your phone

Everything you put into Crumble is kept in the app's private storage, which no other app can read:

  • Your recipes: title, description, ingredients, instructions, times, servings, category, tags, notes, nutrition text, the source link if there is one, and whether it is a favourite.
  • Recipe photos: when you add a photo, Crumble opens Android's own file picker and copies the one image you choose into its private storage. It has no permission to browse your photos or files, and no camera permission.
  • Your settings: theme, your category list, and how the recipe list is sorted and laid out.
  • Whether Pro is unlocked: a single true/false value, cached so the app knows what you own when offline. Google Play remains the authority and is re-checked on every launch.

Crumble has no analytics, no crash reporting and no usage tracking of its own.

Google's advertising, consent and sign-in libraries inside the app keep their own separate state in the app's private storage as well, including your ad-consent choice and, if you use Drive backup, the Google account you signed in with.

Importing a recipe from a web page

When you paste a link and tap import, your phone requests that page directly from the website it belongs to, and then the recipe's main picture if the page names one. Nothing goes through us. The website sees what any website sees when you visit it — your IP address and a browser-style identifier that includes the word "Crumble" — and nothing else about you or your recipe collection.

The page is read on your phone to find the recipe, the picture is saved into the app's private storage, and the link is kept with the recipe as its source. The app only fetches pages you ask it to.

Importing and sharing recipe files

You can open a Crumble or Paprika recipe file in the app, or pick a Paprika export in Settings. The app reads the one file you give it, on your phone.

Sharing a recipe writes it to a temporary file and hands it to Android's share sheet. Where it goes after that is up to you and the app you choose to send it with. The temporary file is cleared the next time you share something.

Google Drive backup (Pro)

Backup is off until you turn it on by signing in with a Google account in Settings. Signing in is handled by Google; Crumble receives your email address and display name, shows them on the Settings screen, and does not send them anywhere.

Crumble asks Google for two permissions: to manage the files it creates itself in your Drive, and to use its own hidden app-data area. It cannot see the rest of your Drive.

When you tap Backup, the app uploads one file — crumble_backup.json, in a folder called "Crumble Backups" in your own Drive — containing all your recipes and their photos. It goes straight from your phone to Google. We never receive it and cannot read it.

The backup file is not encrypted by Crumble. It is protected by your Google account in the same way as anything else in your Drive, and anyone you share that folder with can read it.

Restore downloads that file back to the phone. "Delete backup" removes it from Drive. Signing out in the app stops it using your account on that phone; to withdraw Crumble's access entirely, remove it under "Third-party apps and services" in your Google account. The backup file stays in your Drive until you delete it.

The web viewer

The page at halfurness.com/crumble lets you read a backup in a browser. You sign in with Google and grant it read-only access to your Drive. That permission is broader than the app's — Google offers nothing narrower for reading a file another app created — but the viewer's code only looks for the "Crumble Backups" folder and reads the backup file inside it.

The file is downloaded straight from Google into your browser and displayed there. It is not sent to us; the site is a static page with no server-side code and no analytics. Your sign-in token is kept only for the browser session and is revoked when you sign out. The page loads Google's sign-in script and fonts from Google, so Google sees those requests.

Ads, and what Google's ad SDK collects and shares

If you have not bought Pro, a banner sits along the bottom of the recipe list, served by Google's Mobile Ads SDK (AdMob), which is part of the app.

This is the main place where data about you leaves your phone. It goes to Google, not to us. We receive only aggregated reporting from Google — how many ads were shown and what they earned, broken down by things like country and device — never the individual identifiers or the raw data Google collects. Nothing from your recipes is passed to the ad SDK.

Google publishes the list of what its Mobile Ads SDK collects. Everything on it is both collected by Google and shared with third parties:

  • Your IP address — which Google may use to estimate roughly where your device is.
  • Your interactions with the app — including launches, taps and video views.
  • Diagnostic information — including app launch time, hang rate and energy usage.
  • Device and account identifiers — your Android advertising ID, your App set ID, and other identifiers tied to accounts signed in on the device.

Google says this is used for advertising, analytics, and fraud prevention and security, and that it is encrypted in transit. Of that list, the advertising ID is the one item Google lets an app suppress outright, and Crumble does not suppress it. Crumble also does not request non-personalised ads and does not use Google's limited-ads mode. You can reset or delete your advertising ID yourself in Android's settings.

We cannot delete the data Google collects through the ad SDK, and we cannot retrieve it. It is held by Google under Google's own privacy policy, and any request about it has to go to Google.

Consent in the UK and EEA

If you are in the UK or the EEA, ads cannot be personalised until you have answered a consent form. Crumble uses Google's User Messaging Platform for this, and no ad is requested until Google's SDK reports that requesting one is allowed. Where you have answered before, the app relies on your stored answer rather than rechecking with Google first.

You can change your answer while you are still being shown ads: open Settings and choose "Ad privacy choices". That item is not available once you have bought Pro, because the app no longer contacts Google's consent SDK at all. Buying Pro stops any further ad being requested, but it does not erase an answer you already gave — that stays stored in the app until you clear the app's data or uninstall it.

If you buy Pro

Pro is a single payment, not a subscription, sold through Google Play Billing. It removes the ads, lifts the 50-recipe limit of the free version and unlocks Google Drive backup. The payment happens inside Google Play; Crumble never sees your card details, your name or your billing address. All the app receives is whether this Google account owns the product, which it asks Google Play on every launch — so a refund takes Pro away again, and reinstalling restores it. Recipes you already have are never removed or locked if that happens.

When Pro is unlocked at launch the app does not initialise the ads SDK, does not ask for an ad, and does not show the consent form. A small part of Google's ads library is still started automatically by Android when the app's process starts, because the library registers a start-up component in the app's manifest whether or not ads are ever requested.

Permissions in the installed app

Crumble itself asks for one permission: internet access, which it uses for web import, Drive backup, Google Play and ads. The other permissions in the installed app arrive with Google's advertising and billing libraries — network state, wake-lock, foreground-service, billing and advertising-ID permissions. None of them is used to send anything about your recipes.

Cooking mode can keep the screen on and lets you move between sections with a wave of the hand, using the phone's proximity sensor. The sensor reading is used in the moment and is never stored or sent.

Backups, and moving to a new phone

Crumble is excluded from Android's own cloud backup, so your recipes are not copied to Google that way. A direct phone-to-phone transfer while setting up a new phone may still carry the app's data across; otherwise a new phone starts empty. To move your recipes yourself, use Drive backup and restore, or share them as files. Pro is unaffected — a new install rechecks Google Play and unlocks from your purchase.

Keeping and deleting data

Everything Crumble stores lives in the app's private storage and stays there until you remove it. Deleting a recipe removes it from the app; clearing the app's data or uninstalling the app deletes everything, including your stored ad-consent answer. A Drive backup is yours and stays in your Drive until you delete it, from the app or from Drive itself. There is nothing to delete at our end, because we never receive any of it. Data Google has collected through the ad SDK is Google's to handle, as above.

Children

Crumble is not directed at children. Nothing you enter in the app is ever sent to us. The only data about you that leaves the phone without you asking for it goes to Google, as described above.

Who is responsible for what

For the information held on your phone and in your Drive, you are the one who decides what goes in and what is removed, and we never receive it. For the advertising data described above, Google is the party collecting and using it, under its own privacy policy. In the UK and EEA, personalised advertising relies on the consent you give through the form described above, and you can withdraw it there.

Changes
