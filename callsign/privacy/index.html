Callsign

Privacy policy · last updated 28 September 2026

Callsign is an Android app for the Nothing Phone (4a). It gives each of the six squares on the Glyph Bar a rule naming one or more people. When one of those people messages you, that square lights up until you deal with the notification.

This policy describes Callsign as it is actually built, not how apps of this kind usually work. It is published by Hal Furness.

The short version

  • Deciding which square to light happens entirely on your phone.
  • Callsign never reads the body of a message, and never sends anything about your notifications anywhere.
  • There are no accounts and no server of ours.
  • The app does make two kinds of request to Google: asking Google Play what you have bought, and — unless you have bought Pro — requesting ads. The advertising one collects a substantial amount about your device. It is listed in full below.

Notification access: what Callsign reads

To light a square, Callsign needs to know who a notification is from. You grant that in Android's own Settings, under notification access. Until you do, the app does nothing with your notifications at all.

Be clear about what that permission is. Android hands a notification listener the whole of every notification your phone shows, and lets it dismiss them. That is true of any app you grant it to. What matters is what the app then does. Callsign reads only these fields and ignores the rest:

  • The sender's name, from the messaging fields apps attach to a notification — the name on the most recent message in a thread, and any people the app listed on it.
  • If the app posted no sender information, the notification title, expanded title or conversation title, as a fallback. This fallback applies to notifications from any app, so a name on a square can in principle be matched against something like an email subject line or a headline.
  • The package name of the app that posted it, so a square can be limited to one app.
  • A few technical flags, used to skip notifications that are ongoing (a music player, a download) or that are only a summary of a group.

Callsign never reads the message body. No code in the app looks at the text of a message. Because Android hands over the whole notification, the text does pass through the app's memory in the moment it is delivered — it is simply never read, stored, logged or sent anywhere.

What happens to a notification

The sender's name is compared, in memory, with the names on your squares. The comparison is case-insensitive and matches partial names, so a square for "Sam" will also match "Samantha". The lowest-numbered matching square wins.

The result of that comparison is one thing: a square number. Nothing is written to storage, and no sender name or notification content is ever written to the Android log. The app logs only technical messages about the Glyph connection, Google Play and the ad bar.

While a matched notification is still showing, Callsign holds its Android notification key and which square it lit, in memory only, so it knows which light to turn off later. That is dropped as soon as Android reports the notification gone — usually when you open or dismiss it — and it is lost whenever the service or the phone restarts. When you change a square, the app rescans the notifications currently on screen, not only ones arriving afterwards.

The only thing that leaves the app as a result of a notification is an instruction to Nothing's Glyph service saying which of the six channels to light. It carries no name, no app and nothing about the message.

What is stored on your phone

Callsign's own code writes a single file in the app's private storage, which no other app can read. It holds two things:

  • Your squares: for each one, the square number, the names you typed or picked, whether the light is steady or blinking, and — if you set an app filter — that app's package name and label.
  • Whether Pro is unlocked: a single true/false value, cached so the app knows what you own when offline. Google Play remains the authority and is re-checked on every launch.

No message content, no phone numbers, no email addresses, no contact IDs, no history of who messaged you and when, and no usage statistics.

Google's advertising and consent libraries inside the app keep their own separate state in the app's private storage as well, including your ad-consent choice.

The names you put on a square are usually other people's names, and they have not been asked. They never leave your phone, but they are personal data about someone else, and you are the one entering them. Use whatever form of a name you are comfortable storing.

Picking someone from your contacts

When you tap "Pick from contacts", Callsign does not read your address book. It has no contacts permission at all — READ_CONTACTS is not requested anywhere in the app.

It opens Android's own contact picker instead. Your contacts stay inside that system screen and the app is handed only the one contact you choose, from which it reads a single field: the display name. You can skip the picker and type a name instead; the result is identical.

Limiting a square to one app

A square can be restricted to notifications from one app. To offer that list, Callsign asks Android which installed apps have a launcher icon. It does not use the broad "query all packages" permission. The list is built to fill a drop-down and is not kept; only the app you choose is saved.

Ads, and what Google's ad SDK collects and shares

If you have not bought Pro, a banner sits along the bottom of the app's main screen, served by Google's Mobile Ads SDK (AdMob), which is part of the app.

This is the main place where data about you leaves your phone. It goes to Google, not to us. We receive only aggregated reporting from Google — how many ads were shown and what they earned, broken down by things like country and device — never the individual identifiers or the raw data Google collects.

Google publishes the list of what its Mobile Ads SDK collects. Everything on it is both collected by Google and shared with third parties:

  • Your IP address — which Google may use to estimate roughly where your device is.
  • Your interactions with the app — including launches, taps and video views.
  • Diagnostic information — including app launch time, hang rate and energy usage.
  • Device and account identifiers — your Android advertising ID, your App set ID, and other identifiers tied to accounts signed in on the device.

Google says this is used for advertising, analytics, and fraud prevention and security, and that it is encrypted in transit. Of that list, the advertising ID is the one item Google lets an app suppress outright, and Callsign does not suppress it. Callsign also does not request non-personalised ads and does not use Google's limited-ads mode. You can reset or delete your advertising ID yourself in Android's settings.

We cannot delete the data Google collects through the ad SDK, and we cannot retrieve it. It is held by Google under Google's own privacy policy, and any request about it has to go to Google.

Consent in the UK and EEA

If you are in the UK or the EEA, ads cannot be personalised until you have answered a consent form. Callsign uses Google's User Messaging Platform for this, and no ad is requested until Google's SDK reports that requesting one is allowed. Where you have answered before, the app relies on your stored answer rather than rechecking with Google first.

You can change your answer while you are still being shown ads: open the menu and choose "Ad privacy settings". That item is not available once you have bought Pro, because the app no longer contacts Google's consent SDK at all. Buying Pro stops any further ad being requested, but it does not erase an answer you already gave — that stays stored in the app until you clear the app's data or uninstall it.

If you buy Pro

Pro is a single payment, not a subscription, sold through Google Play Billing. The payment happens inside Google Play; Callsign never sees your card details, your name or your billing address. All the app receives is whether this Google account owns the product, which it asks Google Play on every launch — so a refund takes Pro away again, and reinstalling restores it.

When Pro is unlocked the app does not initialise the ads SDK, does not ask for an ad, and does not show the consent form. A small part of Google's ads library is still started automatically by Android when the app's process starts, because the library registers a start-up component in the app's manifest whether or not ads are ever requested.

Permissions in the installed app

Callsign itself declares almost nothing. Most permissions in the installed app arrive with Google's advertising and billing libraries, along with background components those libraries register — a start-up provider and background job and alarm schedulers. If you inspect the app you will see network, wake-lock, foreground-service, billing and advertising-ID permissions. They belong to those libraries. None of them is used to send anything about your notifications.

Backups, and moving to a new phone

The file holding your squares — and the Pro flag with it — is deliberately excluded from Android's cloud backup and from device-to-device transfer, so the names of the people you watch for do not leave the phone that way. The rest of the app's stored data, which is the Google ad and consent libraries' own settings, is not excluded, so your ad-consent answer can travel to a new phone even though your squares do not.

A new phone therefore starts empty and you re-enter your squares. Notification access also has to be granted again: it is a device-level permission Android does not transfer. Pro is unaffected — a restored install rechecks Google Play and unlocks from your purchase.

Keeping and deleting data

Everything Callsign stores lives in the app's private storage and stays there until you remove it. Clearing the app's data or uninstalling the app deletes all of it, including your squares and your stored ad-consent answer. There is nothing to delete at our end, because we never receive it. Data Google has collected through the ad SDK is Google's to handle, as above.

Children

Callsign is not directed at children and is not designed for them. Nothing you enter in the app — including the names of the people you watch for — is ever sent to us; it stays on your phone. The only data about you that leaves the phone goes to Google, as described above.

Who is responsible for what

For the information held on your phone, you are the one who decides what goes in and what is removed, and we never receive it. For the advertising data described above, Google is the party collecting and using it, under its own privacy policy. In the UK and EEA, personalised advertising relies on the consent you give through the form described above, and you can withdraw it there.
